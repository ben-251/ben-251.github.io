This is an introduction to my new model of art and poetics. So often, we jump to grand ideas of how “geniuses” are made, how to decide whether art is good, or even what activities count as art, with very little justification for our views. The goal of this series of essays is to examine some of the big questions around creating and receiving art. We will begin by defining terms like *artistic expression,* *evaluation,* *craft,* and *resonance*, and then we will construct a solid framework from these. 

Most of my approach here will be philosophical, rather than scientific: I reference the fields of neuroscience, psychology and medicine only when they give useful analogies, not conclusive “evidence” of my claims. In light of this, I will say as a disclaimer that I don’t think of this theory as the *only* meaningful way to think about art; rather, this is one that demonstrates its usefulness by being applicable in a wide range of contexts. 

Some of the most central terms in this series are neologisms (most notably *sonasis*) which, by their nature, give readers no reference point for what the word could mean. There is always a tension in making such decisions, because this creates more difficulty on the part of the reader, who now has to consciously hold the definitions of terms in their mind, before there is a chance they will understand how the terms relate. There is, however, a sense in which this very struggle can be of benefit: this process encourages the reader to engage actively with the arguments, considering terms according my specific meanings, thereby allowing for my argument to be traced successfully. Regardless of whether a term is coined by me, I will include its definition in a glossary page, which will update after each release. 

As for the structure, these essays will fall loosely into three sections:
1. Fundamental definitions
2. Applications to literature
3. Other applications

As far as possible, I have arranged chapters such that they will progress from one section to the next, but in some cases I disrupted this order, either to give clear examples early, or to use an unanswered question as motivation for exploring other ideas. 

Much thanks to anyone who I’ve discussed my thoughts with; if any of my arguments can be described as compelling, it is almost certainly because they have first been tested. To that end, I hope anyone reading this is eager to respond with critique and further discussion.
